# Brief Fingerprint

Use this short decision record before a new frontend composition or an approved
redesign. It prevents a polished but interchangeable screen from replacing a
product-specific one.

## Gather Evidence First

Use only evidence from the user, repository, product copy, existing screens,
design tokens, analytics supplied by the project, or verified runtime behavior.
Unknown evidence stays unknown.

1. State the product job: what must the user understand, decide, or finish?
2. State the audience, content shape, and interaction pressure: occasional
   reader, frequent operator, urgent checkout, dense administration, or another
   observed condition.
3. Name the existing product and brand signals that should remain recognizable.
4. Choose one macrostructure justified by that evidence: guided flow, dense
   workspace, comparison view, reading surface, focused form, or another
   evidence-backed structure.
5. State three product-specific visual signals. They can come from information
   hierarchy, content behavior, a stable design token, a real product object,
   or an interaction pattern. They must not be generic decoration.
6. Reject three generic defaults that would weaken this surface.

## Preserve The Product Shape

For an `audit` or `refine`, preserve existing information architecture unless
the user approves a redesign. Do not use a brief fingerprint to manufacture a
new brand, customer story, visual identity, or product requirement.

## Compact Record

```text
Mode: audit | refine | redesign
Product job: <evidence-backed statement or unknown>
Audience and pressure: <evidence-backed statement or unknown>
Existing signals to preserve: <signals or unknown>
Chosen macrostructure: <one structure and why it fits>
Product-specific visual signals: <three signals>
Generic defaults rejected: <three defaults>
Unknowns: <facts that were not verified>
```

Examples of generic defaults that may be rejected when unsupported: a generic
three-column card grid, decorative gradients, vague dashboard metrics, stock
illustrations, or a theme swap with no workflow improvement.
